# Filled example: a training booklet request

Makmur Lab — Developing worked example — 2026-10-06

Fictional learning example. Every request, option, record, role, and date below is invented. Covered means addressed within this scenario; it does not mean checked in practice.

## Scenario

A fictional learning team needs 20 training booklets by 21 October 2026. Each booklet has 24 A4 monochrome pages, printed duplex on 12 sheets and stapled. The requester supplies a final PDF; a reviewer checks a proof before production.

Learning goal: Separate a common priced scope from an unresolved acceptance question.

Limitations: These invented print requirements illustrate the questions, not a recommended specification, procurement policy, or tested acceptance method.

## Need

Why this matters: Quantity, use, and timing make the need reviewable; role labels give each unanswered question an owner.

**The business or operational problem is stated clearly.**

Covered in scenario: Participants need a paper copy for a fictional training exercise where screens are unavailable.

**Required quantity, timing, and operating conditions are recorded.**

Covered in scenario: 20 booklets; 24 A4 monochrome pages each, duplex and stapled; delivery to the fictional training room by 21 October 2026.

**The person responsible for confirming the technical requirement is identified.**

Covered in scenario: The requester owns PDF content and quantity. The reviewer confirms the agreed print proof.

## Boundary

Why this matters: A quotation should describe the same deliverables, including handoffs that could change the price or schedule.

**Included deliverables are listed.**

Covered in scenario: One proof copy, production of 20 approved booklets, stapling, packaging, and delivery to the training room.

**Exclusions and third-party dependencies are recorded.**

Covered in scenario: Content writing and PDF redesign are excluded. Production depends on the requester supplying the final PDF and the reviewer approving the proof.

**Installation, commissioning, documentation, and training are considered where applicable.**

Not applicable here: No installation or commissioning applies to these booklets. Delivery confirmation is included; running the training session is excluded.

## Acceptance evidence

Why this matters: Listing an inspection is different from agreeing what passes it. The missing criterion stays visible.

**Tests, inspections, or documents that support acceptance are specified.**

Covered in scenario: Compare the proof with the final PDF for page order and page count; count 20 delivered booklets and check stapling.

**Measurement conditions and acceptance ownership are recorded.**

Still open: The reviewer owns acceptance, but the reading conditions and minimum legibility criterion for the proof have not been agreed.

**Uncertain technical requirements are marked for clarification.**

Covered in scenario: Mark paper choice and legibility for clarification on the draft request; do not imply the proof already passed.

## Commercial comparison

Why this matters: Separate options and assumptions so a total price does not hide a different promise.

**Suppliers price a common scope or deviations are separated.**

Covered in scenario: Use the same 20-copy specification and delivery location; ask each fictional option to list deviations separately.

**Optional or conditional work is identified.**

Covered in scenario: Any colour cover or extra proof is an optional line. A changed PDF after proof approval is conditional work.

**Assumptions and commercial exclusions are visible.**

Covered in scenario: Assume one final PDF and one proof round; identify content edits and additional delivery trips as exclusions requiring clarification.

## Change and ownership

Why this matters: An approval point can be proposed while its pricing detail is still unresolved.

**Changes require a defined approval before execution.**

Covered in scenario: The requester approves scope changes in the scenario; the reviewer approves a new proof before changed production starts.

**Additional-work pricing and evidence requirements are defined where uncertainty remains.**

Still open: No price mechanism exists yet for a revised PDF or a second proof. Request a separate basis and record approval before extra work.

**The decision and unresolved questions are recorded.**

Covered in scenario: Decision: keep this as a draft request. Open: paper and legibility criteria, proof turnaround, and treatment of revised files.

## Reflection

Why this matters: Reflection captures what a future trial must check instead of treating completion of boxes as proof of usefulness.

**Resolved questions and remaining gaps**

Resolved: quantity, page format, included delivery, and role ownership. Still open: acceptance conditions and change pricing. In a later sanitized trial, record questions this checklist missed and revise it.

## What remains open

Quantity and included work are clear enough for a draft request. Acceptance of print legibility and the price treatment of a revised PDF remain open; the checklist has not established readiness to order.

Next step: Have the requester and reviewer agree the proof criteria and revision approval before seeking comparable offers.
