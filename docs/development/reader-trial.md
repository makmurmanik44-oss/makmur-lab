# Proposed reader trial — 2026-10-07

Status: prepared, not conducted. This is a practical way to collect reader feedback on the existing Alpha. It is separate from engineering QA and from the field evidence needed to close article gaps. The task wording, timing, and assessment prompts below are editorial proposals, not a validated assessment instrument.

## Set up one session

Use one willing reader and a fictional example. Invite or contact readers only when the project owner chooses to do so; this document does not send invitations. Suggest 15–20 minutes, allow more time where useful, and record prompts instead of hiding assistance. Let the reader use their normal device. Explain that the library is being evaluated; the reader is not being graded.

Keep identifiable feedback and any practical records outside this public repository. Use an anonymous session label such as R1. If notes are later published, review and sanitise them first. There is no feedback form, analytics collection, or account requirement in this trial.

## Try three tasks

| Task given to the reader                                                             | What to observe                                                                                                                                                                                              | Understanding prompt                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| From the homepage, find material that helps compare offers for the same requirement. | Can the reader choose the starting question, reach the existing Atlas guide, and find Scope Clarity and its worksheet/example? Record where they hesitate and any help supplied.                             | Name one delivery or changed-file question from the fictional booklet comparison. Explain why a total alone would be insufficient.                                       |
| Continue to the supplier note and decide what can be concluded from its evidence.    | Can the reader find the next note and distinguish the original filled example from the separate follow-up?                                                                                                   | Explain why the current evidence does not establish a reserved production slot. In the separate Print Shop P scenario, explain the limit of counting two supplier names. |
| Save the supplier note, leave it, then find it again.                                | Does the reader find the save control and Saved notes? Can they reopen the same note after navigation or reload? If storage is unavailable, record the displayed limitation and use the documented fallback. | Explain where the save belongs and why a saved note does not mean the material was validated or understood.                                                              |

Give the task first without showing the route. Use a prompt only after the reader requests help or cannot proceed; record exactly what assistance was needed. Do not infer understanding merely from reaching a page or checking a box.

## Decide what follows

Separate navigation friction from concept misunderstanding. If the reader cannot find the worksheet, review its entry and labels. If they reach it but treat a general capacity statement as confirmed supply, review the explanation and example. A technical storage limit should be recorded separately from either conclusion.

Make a small revision linked to the observation, then check that specific problem again. Keep the original observation and the reason for the change so improvement claims can be traced. If no reader trial has occurred, report the guide as prepared rather than completed.

Reader feedback may improve clarity, but it does not close `practice-validation` or `continuity-evidence`. Practical closure still requires the [editorial review workflow](content-review.md): safely sanitised observed evidence, a human reviewer, limits, and a documented revision. Retain the Learning/Developing status while those gaps remain.
