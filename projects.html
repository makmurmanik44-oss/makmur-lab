Work presented as problems, systems, and lessons.

The purpose of these case studies is not to expose confidential company information. It is to show the reasoning structure behind the work.

CASE 01 · ANALYTICS

Procurement Control Tower

Procurement data often exists, but decision visibility is fragmented. This project turns operational data into a single view of suppliers, spend, contracts, open PO, activity, and lifecycle.

PROBLEM

Information is distributed across raw PO data, supplier lists, contract records, and manual follow-up.

APPROACH

Build a single-source structure linking vendor master, spend analytics, contract lifecycle, PO status, and supplier activity.

OUTCOME

A clearer decision layer for category review, supplier concentration, open commitments, and follow-up priorities.

MY ROLE

Problem framing, data structure design, workflow logic, dashboard requirements, and iterative testing.

TOOLS

Google Sheets, Apps Script, structured master data, dashboard logic, and automation.

LESSON

A dashboard is useful only when the underlying data ownership and definitions are disciplined.

CASE 02 · GOVERNANCE

Procurement Quality Gates

Many procurement failures are created before commercial negotiation begins: unclear scope, ambiguous acceptance criteria, missing ownership, or execution before approval.

PROBLEM

Procurement can become responsible for outcomes created by upstream ambiguity.

APPROACH

Define quality checks at requirement, tender, clarification, award, execution, acceptance, and closure stages.

OUTCOME

Clearer accountability, fewer interpretation gaps, and stronger auditability of key decisions.

MY ROLE

Translate repeated operational issues into governance questions, controls, and standard checkpoints.

TOOLS

RACI, scope matrix, MoM, technical-commercial comparison, approval rules, and acceptance criteria.

LESSON

Governance should reduce decision ambiguity without becoming unnecessary bureaucracy.

CASE 03 · VENDOR MANAGEMENT

Supplier Lifecycle System

A long supplier list does not automatically mean healthy supplier coverage. The important question is who is active, strategic, under-used, duplicated, or creating concentration risk.

PROBLEM

Supplier count alone hides dormant vendors, one-time vendors, category gaps, and concentration risk.

APPROACH

Classify suppliers by activity, category, spend, recency, onboarding age, and actual utilization.

OUTCOME

Better basis for supplier rationalization, sourcing priorities, category coverage, and performance review.

MY ROLE

Define segmentation logic and integrate it into broader procurement visibility.

TOOLS

Vendor master, activity aging, spend segmentation, category mapping, and lifecycle tracking.

LESSON

Supplier resilience depends on usable capability, not merely the number of names in a database.

CASE 04 · PERSONAL SYSTEM

Quest OS

Work produces experience, but experience does not automatically become learning. Quest OS is an experiment to make execution, evidence, lessons, and progress visible.

PROBLEM

Tasks get completed but lessons, evidence, and reusable patterns can disappear.

APPROACH

Connect work master, subtasks, missions, evidence, lesson learned, and achievements into one loop.

OUTCOME

A more deliberate personal operating system for execution and continuous improvement.

MY ROLE

System owner, workflow designer, user, tester, and continuous improver.

TOOLS

Google Apps Script, Sheets, workflow logic, scoring, evidence, and review rhythm.

LESSON

A productivity system fails when maintaining the system becomes heavier than doing the work.
